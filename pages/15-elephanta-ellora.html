STOP 15

Elephanta & Ellora

If you have visited Ellora, this is a particularly useful connection.

UNESCO notes that the plan of Cave One at Elephanta closely resembles Dumar Lena at Ellora.

That does not mean the two monuments are identical.

They are not.

But they belong to the same broader history of Indian rock-cut architecture.

Elephanta's Great Cave is a rock-cut temple excavated into the hill.

Ellora contains Buddhist, Hindu and Jain caves, developed over a much larger chronological span.

And then there is the Kailasa temple at Ellora, an extraordinary monument excavated from the rock in a different technique.

At Elephanta, the interior hall and sanctum dominate your experience.

At Ellora's Kailasa, the mountain has been transformed into an enormous freestanding architectural complex.

When you compare them, the same basic genius becomes visible:

Indian architects learned to make stone behave like architecture.

Walls could become figures.

Pillars could emerge from rock.

A mountain could become a temple.
