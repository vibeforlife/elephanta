ELEPHANTA GUIDE
STOP 16

What to Photograph & Visual Game

You don't need a thousand photographs.

Take a few deliberate ones.

First, photograph the overall interior of Cave One.

Second, photograph Sadashiva straight on.

Third, take a side-angle image of Sadashiva so you can see the depth.

Fourth, photograph one of the pillars and its capital.

Fifth, photograph Yogishvara and Nataraja if the crowd allows.

Sixth, photograph Ardhanarishvara.

Seventh, photograph Gangadhara.

Eighth, photograph Kalyanasundara.

Ninth, photograph Ravana under Kailash.

Tenth, photograph the central shrine.

And take one photograph looking back through the cave toward the entrance.

That last photograph is important.

It captures the relationship between darkness, architecture and light.

And please don't sacrifice your physical experience for the perfect photograph.

The photograph can be found online.

Your actual visit cannot.

Here's a useful game to play while you walk.

For every major sculpture, ask:

What is Shiva doing?

Then ask:

What is Shiva controlling?

Yogishvara.

He controls himself.

Nataraja.

He controls movement.

Gangadhara.

He controls the Ganges.

Ravana.

He controls the mountain.

Andhakasuravadha.

He controls destructive force.

Ardhanarishvara.

He contains complementary principles in one form.

Kalyanasundara.

He participates in social and divine union.

Sadashiva.

He contains multiple aspects within one monumental identity.

Now ask one final question:

Where is the sacred centre?

The linga shrine.

That question makes the entire cave click into place.
