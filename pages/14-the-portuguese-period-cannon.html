ELEPHANTA GUIDE
STOP 14

The Portuguese Period & Cannon

Now think about the island in the early modern period.

The Portuguese encountered the caves and the island became part of their colonial world.

The famous elephant sculpture near the landing area contributed to the name Elephanta.

The island also preserves a cannon associated with the Portuguese period.

That cannon is useful because it is visually obvious.

It tells you:

“This place had a military and colonial history too.”

But remember what we said earlier.

Do not turn the cannon into a universal explanation for every damaged sculpture.

History is messier.

The caves survived changing political powers, changing religious practices, environmental exposure and centuries of visitors.

The fact that the core architecture remains at all is extraordinary.

If you climb high enough to reach the cannon, stop for the view.

Look across the harbour.

This is one of the best moments to understand the geography of the site.

You are on an island close to one of India's largest modern cities.

The ancient temple is hidden inside the hill.

The sea surrounds you.

And the city sits across the water.

The contrast is almost cinematic.

Now imagine the island without skyscrapers.

No modern harbour.

No high-rises.

No container ships.

No smartphones.

The cave would have stood in a radically different visual environment.

But the essential relationship remains.

Island.

Hill.

Rock.

Cave.

Sea.

That geography is part of the monument's identity.
