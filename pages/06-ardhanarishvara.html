ELEPHANTA GUIDE
STOP 06

Ardhanarishvara

Now move to Ardhanarishvara.

Stop close enough to see the division of the body but far enough to see the whole composition.

The figure combines Shiva and Parvati in one body.

The masculine and feminine are represented within one unified form.

Look at the body.

Look at the ornament.

Look at the different visual cues used to distinguish the two sides.

This is not merely a clever artistic trick.

It expresses a major religious idea.

Shiva and Shakti are complementary.

The divine can be understood through the relationship between masculine and feminine principles.

And again, look at the physical challenge.

The sculptor has to make the division visible.

But if the division becomes too strong, the figure stops looking like one being.

If the unity becomes too strong, the distinction disappears.

The success of the image depends on maintaining both.

Now compare Ardhanarishvara with Sadashiva.

Sadashiva gives you multiplicity within Shiva.

Ardhanarishvara gives you complementarity within one body.

The cave is constantly making philosophical ideas physical.

As you continue around the cave, notice that Parvati is not merely an accessory to Shiva.

She appears in several major stories.

That matters.

The Elephanta program is not only about an isolated male ascetic deity.

It includes marriage, family, divine feminine power and the relationship between Shiva and Parvati.

This becomes especially important in the Kalyanasundara scene.

But even before we reach it, keep your eyes open for how the artists handle the goddess.

The sculptures are damaged, and identifying individual secondary figures can sometimes be difficult.

That is one of the reasons it is dangerous to invent precise identifications for every small figure.

Where the evidence is strong, we will name the figure.

Where it isn't, we will say so.
