STOP 08

Ravana Under Kailash

Now we get one of the cave's most dramatic stories.

Find Ravana under Mount Kailash.

Ravana is a legendary king of Lanka from the Ramayana tradition.

He is immensely powerful.

He is also famously proud.

In the story represented here, Ravana attempts to shake or lift Mount Kailash, the sacred mountain associated with Shiva.

Imagine the confidence.

Ravana is essentially saying:

“I can move the mountain.”

Shiva's response is wonderfully economical.

He presses it down.

Ravana is trapped beneath it.

The scene is therefore about much more than physical strength.

It is about the limits of ego.

Ravana is powerful.

But Shiva is operating at another level.

Look at the composition.

Try to see the relationship between the mountain, Shiva and Ravana.

The sculptor has created a hierarchy.

The mountain is enormous.

Ravana struggles below.

Shiva controls the entire situation.

And now notice something important about the story.

Ravana is not necessarily presented as a simple cartoon villain.

In broader Hindu tradition, he can be a complicated figure: powerful, learned, devoted to Shiva, and simultaneously capable of enormous arrogance.

That complexity makes the story more interesting.

The lesson is not simply:

“Bad guy gets punished.”

It is:

Power without humility can become absurd.

And the story has another connection that may be especially interesting if you have travelled in Maharashtra.

At Ellora, the great Kailasa temple contains a famous Ravana-under-Kailasa depiction.

So when you eventually visit or revisit Ellora, look for the visual conversation between the two monuments.

At Elephanta, the entire temple is excavated into the rock.

At Ellora's Kailasa, an enormous temple was excavated from the rock mass in a radically different architectural operation.

Both show how Indian builders turned mountains into sacred architecture.
