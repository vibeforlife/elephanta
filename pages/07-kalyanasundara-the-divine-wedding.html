STOP 07

Kalyanasundara — The Divine Wedding

Now find Kalyanasundara.

This is the marriage of Shiva and Parvati.

The mood changes immediately.

The cave has just shown you cosmic river control and violent demon combat.

Now we get a wedding.

This is one of the great things about Indian mythology.

It refuses to put gods into one emotional box.

Shiva can be terrifying.

Shiva can be meditative.

Shiva can be romantic.

And all of these belong to the same religious universe.

Look carefully at the central figures.

Shiva and Parvati are shown as a pair.

Around them are other participants in the divine ceremony.

This is a social scene.

That is important.

The yogi who sits alone is now a husband.

The ascetic has entered a relationship.

The divine has become domestic.

And yet the marriage has cosmic significance.

Parvati's union with Shiva is not merely a wedding between two characters.

It represents the joining of major divine principles.

Now compare this with Ardhanarishvara.

In Ardhanarishvara, Shiva and Parvati are one body.

In Kalyanasundara, they are two beings united through marriage.

Different visual language.

Related religious idea.
